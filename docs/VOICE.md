# Parley voice guide — read before writing ANY message text

People don't text like an English textbook. Every message in Parley — samples, drills, demo case, landing examples,
and the replies we suggest — must read like a real phone, not a worksheet.

## 1. How the other parent texts (their side)
- **Bursts, not letters.** One "message" is usually 2–4 bubbles sent seconds or minutes apart, sometimes a follow-up
  much later ("hello??" at 10:14 after nothing since 9:41). Model it as an ordered list of bubbles, each with a time.
- **Lowercase, little punctuation, run-ons.** "ur late again" · "so ur not coming" · "i literally told u this yesterday"
- **Texting shorthand — use a wide mix, not the same five:** u, ur, r, ik, idk, idc, ikr, rn, tbh, ngl, fr, istg, smh, omw, wya, wyd, hbu, lmk, nvm, ofc, wtv/whatev, bc/cuz/bcuz, prob/prolly, tmrw/tmr, tn (tonight), wknd, rq, pls/plz, ty/thx, k/kk, ok?, ya/yea/yeah/nah, gonna/wanna/gotta/kinda, bout, 'bout, w/ (with), b4, 2 (to/too) occasionally, lol / lmao / lmfao (often passive-aggressive), imo, dw, np, "seriously", "unreal", "wow", "bruh".
  Meaning matters: `lmk` / `lmk asap` = a real ASK; `ik` / `ikr` = "I know"; `wtv` / `k.` / `fine.` = dismissive;
  `istg` = escalation; `nvm` = they withdrew the ask (or are guilt-tripping); `dw` = "don't worry" (often sarcastic).
- **Emphasis by CAPS and punctuation:** "AGAIN", "???", "!!", "…", "hello??", "answer me", "?" alone as a bubble.
- **Emoji, sparingly and pointed:** 🙄 🙃 😂 🤦‍♀️ 👍 (a lone 👍 or "k." can be hostile).
- **Typos happen** (dont, cant, im, thier, alot) — not in every message, but some.
- **Personas vary** — don't make them all the same angry voice:
  - *The rapid-fire one:* 3–4 short bubbles, caps, "??".
  - *The cold one:* "k." / "noted." / "fine. whatever."
  - *The sugar-coated one:* "Just a friendly reminder 🙂 that SOME of us pay for soccer on time"
  - *The midnight essay:* one long run-on paragraph at 1:12am that drags up the past and buries one real ask.
  - *The kid-as-messenger one:* "emma says u told her im the reason…"
  - *The logistics-only one with a sting:* "pickup 5. dont be late this time"
- Kids' names, real places (school lot, Target parking lot, grandma's), real times (5:30, after practice).
- Never cartoonish, never slurs, mild swearing at most ("wtf", "damn", "pissed"), no violence.

## 2. How OUR suggested replies sound (our side)
A relaxed, decent adult texting their co-parent — **not a lawyer, not HR, not a support bot.** Read it out loud: if
nobody would actually text that, rewrite it.
- **Mirror their register.** Friendly or casual message → casual, friendly reply: "hey, not much! anything you need for
  the kids this week?" · "yep that works" · "ok cool, i'll grab them at 5" · "no worries, I'll bring it Sat".
  Lowercase starts, "hey", "yeah", "yep", "cool", "no worries" are all fine. One "!" is fine in a friendly reply.
- **Hostile message → still calm and short, but human, never stiff.** "On my way, 10 min out." · "Got it. I'll have
  them ready at 5." Open with the answer. No "!" when they were tense.
- Neutral shorthand is ok: **ok, thx, tmrw, rn, b4, w/**. Nothing else — never "u", "ur", "r", "pls", "idk", "bc",
  "omw", "lol", "lmao", "smh", in any reply (a judge has to read it without a decoder).
- Contractions always (I'll, can't, that's). Plain words. 1–3 short sentences. Brief ≤ 20 words.
- **Never:** "I acknowledge", "Per our agreement" (unless quoting the plan matters), "Kindly", "Please be advised",
  "As previously stated", "Moving forward", "I hope this message finds you", "I understand your frustration",
  "Thank you for your message", "Regarding", "In regards to", "Is there something you need…", "Please let me know if…".
- No emoji, no jabs, no sarcasm, no admissions ("sorry", "my bad", "it won't happen again") unless the user wrote it.
- Never curt-hostile either: no bare "k" or "Noted." alone as the whole reply (reads cold). "Noted, thanks." is ok in gray rock.
- Answer the burst ONCE. Don't reply bubble by bubble. Skip the jabs; answer the one real ask.
- Concrete: times, places, amounts — or [placeholders] when we don't know them.

Robotic → human:
  "Hi, not much here. Is there something you need about the kids?" → "hey, not much! anything you need for the kids this week?"
  "I acknowledge receipt of your message regarding pickup." → "Got it, I'll be there at 5."
  "Thank you for picking them up today." → "thx for grabbing them!" (when they were friendly first)

## 3. How OUR explanations sound (everything else the AI writes to the user)
adviceWhy, "just the facts", asks, the judge's-lens why/fix, drill feedback, the "better" reply's notes: talk like a
smart friend who's on their side. Second person, short, plain, no jargon.
- "Nothing to answer here — they're just saying hi. Keep it light."
- "They want to know if you're coming Sat at 10."
- "You only need to tell them when you'll get there. Skip the rest."
- Lens why: "A judge would read this as a threat, even if you're just venting." · fix: "Cut it." / "Say when you'll pay instead."
- **Never:** "It's a friendly hello, so a short, polite reply is enough." (a form letter), "The other parent states…",
  "This message does not require a response", "It is advisable to…", "in order to".
- One sentence where one will do. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no therapy-speak.
- **Records are the exception in tone, not in plainness.** The lawyer/month/pattern report bullets, the incident title
  and note, and the journal's "Make it neutral" stay neutral and factual (no opinions, no labels), but in plain everyday
  English: "Pickup started 40 minutes late." — never "The respondent failed to arrive at the designated time."
  No legalese: "the minor child", "subsequently", "proceeded to", "stated that", "pursuant to", "aforementioned".

## 4. Wrong options in practice drills
They must be what a stressed parent *really* sends: "lmao ok", "u literally never pay on time so", "omg here we go",
"maybe if u actually showed up for once", "wow. ok.", a 4-line over-explanation, "my lawyer will hear about this",
"fine take them then 🙄". Tempting, human, realistic — not cartoon villains.
In a drill all THREE options are texted the same way — lowercase starts, "ok", "yep", "fyi", light shorthand
(u, thx, tmrw, b4, w/) in any of them, the good one included — or the good one is the only tidy sentence and gets
picked by its tone. At most one wrong option per question is openly heated; the other sounds calm and is wrong in
content (gives in, accepts the blame, no time, answers a different thing, a polite dig). Week 4: no heated ones.

## 5. Data shape
- A burst is `string[]` (bubbles in order). Optional times as `{text, at}` where shown.
- Pasted text: blank lines OR one-message-per-line chat exports become separate bubbles; a single paragraph stays one bubble.
- In the UI, render a burst as stacked chat bubbles (left side, grey), small time under the last one; our reply as one
  bubble on the right (blue).
