# Parley: the 28-day calm co-parenting course

Each day: a 1-minute lesson (one idea, one real-sounding text, the calm way), a 2-minute practice, and an optional "try it today" task for the next real message. Verdicts are in words only, never numbers. The course teaches how to communicate; it is not legal advice, not therapy, and not a court-ordered parenting class. Parley is not a law firm and doesn't give legal advice.

**Practice types**
- **Pick**: the existing drill. Five short bursts from the other parent; pick the reply you'd send. Each pick gets a one-line verdict in words ("Answers the ask", "Takes the bait").
- **Spot**: tap the line in a burst that matters (the real ask, the bait, the threat, the one fact).
- **Rewrite**: write your own reply or fix a hot one. The verdict is a few words on what it does and the one thing to cut.
- **Sort**: drag messages into piles (answer now / wait / no reply needed; reply / record only).
- **Pause**: a short calming routine before answering a hard text (breathe, name the feeling, wait). A technique, not treatment.
- **Reflect**: a words-only check-in. No grades.

## Week 1 · Stop the spiral
*You stop feeding the fight. Replies get shorter, calmer and about one thing.*

| Day | Title | The idea in one line | Practice | Try it today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why replies blow up | A hot reply gets a hotter one back; the calm one gives the fight nothing to hold. | Pick | Before your next reply, ask "will this end it or feed it?" |
| 2 | Pause before you answer | The urge to answer fast is the feeling talking; ten minutes is almost always fine. | Pause | Wait ten minutes before answering the next tense text. |
| 3 | Answer the ask, not the jab | Most angry texts hide one real question; answer that and leave the rest alone. | Spot, then Pick | Find the ask in the next message before you type a word. |
| 4 | Brief and factual | A few short lines with the facts that matter, then stop. | Rewrite | Cut your next reply in half before you send it. |
| 5 | Drop "always" and "never" | Absolutes start arguments about the past instead of plans for this week. | Rewrite | Catch one "always" or "never" in a draft and swap it for the fact. |
| 6 | One message, not five | Say it once, in one message, and let the silence do the rest. | Rewrite | Send one message and don't follow it up for an hour. |
| 7 | When not to answer at all | Not every text needs a reply; some just need to be read and kept. | Sort, then Reflect | Leave one message that asks nothing without an answer. |

## Week 2 · Don't take the bait
*You see the hook before you bite, and you get boring on purpose.*

| Day | Title | The idea in one line | Practice | Try it today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Spotting bait | Bait is the line written to make you defend yourself; you don't have to. | Spot | Name the bait in the next message, silently, then skip it. |
| 9 | Guilt trips | "I guess the kids just don't matter to you" is a feeling, not a question; answer the plan. | Pick | Reply to the logistics in a guilt-trip text and nothing else. |
| 10 | The past dragged up | Old fights don't get settled by text; keep today's message about today. | Rewrite | When the past comes up, answer only the part about this week. |
| 11 | The new partner | Comments about a new partner are rarely about logistics; stay on the kids. | Pick | Leave a new-partner remark unanswered and reply to the rest. |
| 12 | Kids used as messengers | When news comes "from the kids", answer the adult and keep the kids out of it. | Spot, then Pick | Next time a kid carries a message, check it directly with the other parent. |
| 13 | Threats ("I'll take you to court") | Don't threaten back or argue the law by text; answer the logistics and keep the message. If you have a lawyer, that's who to ask. | Pick | Save the next threat to your record and reply to the plan only. |
| 14 | Boring on purpose | The flat, polite, dull reply (some call it gray rock) gives the fight nothing to feed on. | Rewrite, then Reflect | Make one reply so boring there is nothing to argue with. |

## Week 3 · Boundaries without a fight
*You ask clearly, say no kindly and stick to what was agreed.*

| Day | Title | The idea in one line | Practice | Try it today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | A clear ask with a time | "Can you confirm by Thursday 6pm?" gets an answer; "we need to talk" gets a fight. | Rewrite | Put a day and a time in your next request. |
| 16 | Saying no calmly | A short no with one reason and one option beats a long no with five reasons. | Pick | Say no to one request in two sentences or fewer. |
| 17 | Schedule changes | Confirm the change, the day, the time and who drives, all in one message. | Rewrite | Confirm the next schedule change in writing, with the time. |
| 18 | Money and expenses | Amount, what it was for, a photo of the receipt, a date. Nothing about the past. | Rewrite | Send one expense request with the receipt and nothing else. |
| 19 | Handoffs | Logistics only at pickup and drop-off: where, when, what to bring. | Pick | Keep the next handoff text to the where and when. |
| 20 | When they don't answer | One calm follow-up with a time, then decide what you'll do; no chasing. | Sort | Send one follow-up, then stop until the time you named. |
| 21 | Point to what you agreed | Quote the schedule you both have instead of arguing about it. You don't need to interpret it. | Pick, then Reflect | When a plan is disputed, quote the agreed schedule word for word. |

## Week 4 · Kids first, record kept
*You protect the kids from the conflict and keep a calm, dated record.*

| Day | Title | The idea in one line | Practice | Try it today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 22 | Talking to your kids about two homes | Simple, honest, and never about who's to blame. | Pick (what you'd say to your kid) | Tell your kid one good thing about their time at the other home. |
| 23 | Never vent to the kids | Kids hear "your mom" or "your dad" as half of themselves; vent to an adult instead. | Sort | Notice one moment you wanted to vent near the kids, and take it elsewhere. |
| 24 | Keeping a calm record | Date, what happened, what was said, in plain words. No adjectives. | Rewrite (a record note) | Save one message to your record with a one-line note. |
| 25 | What a lawyer actually needs | Dates, facts, the messages themselves, short and in order. Not your feelings about them. Parley doesn't give legal advice; your lawyer does. | Sort (keep / leave out) | Pick the three messages you'd show first, and why, in one line each. |
| 26 | Preparing for mediation | Go in with your calm habits: one topic at a time, the ask, the time, and options. | Pick | Write down the two things you most want to agree on, in one line each. |
| 27 | Staying calm long-term | The skills hold when they're habits: pause, find the ask, keep it short. | Pause | Use the pause on the next hard text without the app. |
| 28 | Your 28-day review | Your Day 1 reply next to today's, and the skills you now use without thinking. | Rewrite (the Day 1 message again), then Reflect | Pick one skill to keep practising next month. |

## Guardrails this follows
- Education about communication only. No state-specific legal instructions, no predicted outcomes, no promise about custody.
- Never "court-approved", never presented as a parenting class a court might order.
- Calming techniques only; no claim to treat anxiety or trauma.
- The "brief, factual, friendly, firm" idea is described in our own words and never named after a trademarked method.
- No numeric scores. Streak days and "Day 3 of 28" are progress, not grades.
