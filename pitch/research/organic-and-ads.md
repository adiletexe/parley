So the hook has to be the *drama* (the ex's texts, narcissists, family court). "BIFF" and "gray rock" belong inside the content as the payoff, not in the title.

### Formats that work (observed on the #highconflictcoparenting Instagram feed)
Parallel-parenting explainers, "document everything for court", custody-schedule takes, stepparent emotional labour, and coach posts with "comment SCRIPT for my template" calls to action. **Most of the top posts sell scripts, guides or coaching.** That means people in this niche already pay for reply scripts, which supports Parley's model.

### Are these videos shared and saved?
- I found **no public data on shares or saves for co-parenting content specifically.**
- Platform-level evidence that shares matter: Adam Mosseri (head of Instagram) named watch time, likes and **sends per reach** as the three most important ranking signals (Jan 2025), with sends "slightly more important for unconnected content" (reaching people who don't follow you). Social Media Today: https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/ ; timeline: https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/unpacking-instagrams-focus-on-sends-per-reach-a-timeline-why-it-matters-and-tips-for-creators-brands
- Our guess, not measured: a "look what my ex sent me" post gets DM'd to the one friend who knows the ex. That is exactly the action this ranking signal rewards.

### Evidence against
- Average views per post are modest, 11K–30K, the same as TikTok's average. The niche has big numbers because there are many posts, not because each post gets many views.
- Gray rock and BIFF are small as search terms.
- There is a backlash: a family-law forum argues that "grey rock is not a legal strategy" and that the method was sold to parents by "divorce influencers": https://www.ottawadivorce.com/forum/threads/grey-rock-is-not-a-legal-strategy-the-hidden-collapse-vector-in-family-court.23238/
- NarcTok is often criticised for misinformation (Men's Health AU, Deseret). That is a brand-safety risk if Parley leans on "narcissist" framing.
- Posting an ex's real texts carries privacy and defamation risk, and family courts can look badly on a parent who does it. Parley's content should use made-up or reenacted texts.

## (b) Can people share our ads? Yes on Meta, and Spark Ads on TikTok

- **Instagram Reels ads (Meta's official ad spec):** viewers can "comment, like, view, save, share and skip" Reels ads the same way they can organic Reels. https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/image/instagram-reels (I read the RU version of the page; the wording is the same.)
- **Facebook Reels ads:** in Meta's newsroom, Feb 2022: "Just like with organic content on Facebook, people can comment, like, view, save, share and skip them." https://about.fb.com/news/2022/02/launching-facebook-reels-globally/
- **Feed ads:** Ads Manager reports a **Post shares** metric for ads ("people can share ads or posts on their own or friends' Timelines, in groups, and on their own Pages", DashThis's summary of the metric): https://help.dashthis.com/meta-ads-metrics-and-dimensions. Instagram feed ads show the paper-plane share/DM icon (third-party guide): https://graphed.com/blog/how-to-share-instagram-ad-via-messenger. I did **not** find a Meta help article that says this directly for feed ads. The ad spec above only covers Reels.
- **TikTok Spark Ads (official help center):** Spark Ads run from organic posts, so "all views, comments, shares, likes, and follows gained from boosting the video" are credited to the organic post. https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/spark-ads?lang=en
- **TikTok non-Spark in-feed ads:** they run under a placeholder account. TikTok's comparison table lists only clicks (CTA, caption, profile) for them, and third-party guides disagree on whether they can be shared. **Plan on Spark Ads if we want shares.** https://dynamoi.com/learn/tiktok-music-promotion/tiktok-spark-ads-vs-non-spark-ads
- **Change to attribution (secondary source, check it):** Meta used to count likes, shares and saves as "clicks" for click-through attribution. A DiG Growth post says that from **3 Mar 2026** only link clicks count. https://ppc.land/advertisers-discover-meta-counts-likes-and-shares-as-clicks-for-conversions ; https://diggrowth.com/blogs/marketing-attribution/share-attribution-on-facebook/

**What this means:** every share of a paid ad is reach we don't pay for. Once someone forwards it, the friend who receives it sees it as a message from a friend, not as an ad. A Spark Ad also keeps its likes and shares on our organic post after the campaign ends, so paid spend builds up the account's own social proof. Nothing published gives a "free reach per share" ratio, so any figure for that on a slide is our own estimate.

Counterpoint: the incumbents' business-to-professional distribution (courts ordering the app) is a moat that organic content cannot copy. Looking old-fashioned on Instagram does not mean they are losing.

## (d) Meta ad policy constraints

- **Personal attributes policy** (official): ads must not assert or imply the viewer's "race, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, age, sexual orientation or practices, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health (including medical conditions), vulnerable financial status, voting status, membership in a trade union, criminal record, or name." **Marital or divorce status is not on the list.** https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/
  - Even so, "Divorced and lonely?" is listed as a typical rejection by a third-party compliance site: https://www.auditsocials.com/knowledge/rejections/personal-attributes. Meta's reviewers judge the "you + personal trait" pattern broadly, and copy that implies the viewer is in an abusive relationship or has a mental-health problem *is* covered by the list. **Safe pattern:** describe the situation, not the person ("When the text says 'you're a terrible mother'…", "Co-parenting texts, answered calmly"). Avoid "Are you divorced?" and "Is your ex a narcissist?".
- **Discriminatory practices** (official): **family status** is a protected characteristic. That applies to excluding people, not to talking about co-parenting. https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/unacceptable-content/discriminatory-practices/
- **Special Ad Categories:** there are four: Housing, Employment, Financial products & services (formerly Credit), and Social issues/elections/politics. **Family, divorce and legal services are not one of them.** https://www.jonloomer.com/special-ad-categories-meta-ads/ (updated 1 Mar 2025); https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/ads-commerce/marketing-api/audiences/special-ad-category. One law-marketing blog claims legal services must run under a Special Ad Category; nothing official I found supports that. Note the risk that a very political ad about family-court reform could be flagged as a "social issue".
- **Lawyer ads:** no category-wide Meta restriction. Meta does remove ads that recruit plaintiffs against Meta itself (reported 9 Apr 2026) and ads about health-related lawsuits in a "sensational or misleading way": https://www.legalexaminer.com/lestaffer/technology/meta-stops-client-recruitment-ads-for-social-media-addiction-lawsuits/. Parley is not a law firm, so state bar advertising rules (ABA Model Rules 7.1/7.2) don't apply to us. But "court-safe" and "court-admissible" are claims, and we should be able to back them up or soften them ("built for a judge to read").
- **Targeting after the Jan 2022 removals:** Meta removed thousands of detailed-targeting options on **19 Jan 2022**, in health, race and ethnicity, politics, religion and sexual orientation (Search Engine Land): https://searchengineland.com/meta-will-remove-targeting-options-for-sensitive-topics-on-january-19-378095 ; https://www.jonloomer.com/facebook-removed-thousands-of-detailed-targeting-options/. Relationship status was not named as removed. **Unverified:** whether "Divorced" or "Separated" are still selectable. Older guides list them, and 2026 guides only list single, in a relationship, engaged and married (https://lineardesign.com/blog/facebook-detailed-targeting-list-2024/). Parental-status targeting (parents by child age) still exists per 2026 guides. **Check this in Ads Manager before the pitch.** In practice: broad targeting or Advantage+ audiences, with the creative doing the targeting (a hostile-text screenshot finds the person living it), plus interests (e.g. Dr. Ramani, co-parenting pages) where they are still available.

**Gaps:** I found **no public test of Notes-app, screenshot or handwritten ads specifically** with a CTR uplift. RevenueCat (11 May 2026) says ugly ads "frequently" win creative tests but gives no numbers: https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/direct-response-creative. The Billo benchmark (80,069 Meta video ads, ~$105M spend, H2 2025) warns that **thumb-stop rate does not predict sales**: https://s3.amazonaws.com/media.mediapost.com/uploads/BILLO_Benchmarking_2026_05.pdf. A native-looking ad gets people to stop scrolling, but whether it converts still has to be tested.

**Why it fits Parley:** the product's raw material *is* a screenshot (a hostile text, and a calm reply under it). A fake iMessage thread or Notes-app draft looks exactly like what the viewer already gets on their phone. It is both the native format and the product demo. Motion's finding that text-only assets have the highest hit rate (11.6%) is the most directly relevant number.
